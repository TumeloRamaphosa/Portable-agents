## 1. The 7kg case: the registry closed a real defect with wrong arithmetic

**This is a live money bug and it is still open.** The price registry marks item 2 in "OPEN ITEMS FOR
OWNER CONFIRMATION" as **"~~7kg case vs 1kg~~ — ✅ CLOSED — NOT AN ERROR"**, on the reasoning:

> "R1,786/kg vs R1,450/kg is a **~19% volume discount**, which is normal wholesale behaviour. Our
> pages already match the store exactly. **No change needed.**"

**The arithmetic is wrong, and the conclusion inverts.**

The registry itself owns these prices (`studex-meat/PRICE-SOURCE-REGISTRY.md`, §2.1):

| Product | Registry owns | Live store charges | Per-kg at owned price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wagyu Biltong Gold, 1kg | **R1,250** | R1,450 | R1,250/kg |
| Wagyu Biltong Gold, 7kg case | **R8,400** | R12,500 | R1,200/kg |

Two separate errors in one sentence:

1. **Direction.** R1,786/kg is **not** cheaper than R1,450/kg — it is **23.2% MORE** per kg. A
   customer buying one 7kg case pays **R2,350 more** than buying seven 1kg bags (R12,500 vs
   7 × R1,450 = R10,150). That is a *premium*, not a discount, and "normal wholesale behaviour" is
   the opposite of what the number says.
2. **Arithmetic.** "~19%" does not match either figure. 12,500 ÷ 7 ÷ 1,450 = **1.232**. The
   registry's own number (R1,786/kg vs R1,450/kg) is a **23.2% premium**, so the "~19%" is not a
   rounding of it either.

**And the prices being compared were the wrong ones.** The registry owns R8,400 and R1,250. At
those prices the case is **R1,200/kg — a genuine 4.0% discount** versus seven 1kg bags (R8,400 vs
7 × R1,250 = R8,750, i.e. **R350 cheaper**). So:

- At the prices the registry **owns**: the case **beats** seven bags by 4%. ✔ the ladder is sound.
- At the prices the **store charges** (R12,500 / R1,450): the case is **23.2% dearer**. ✘

🔑 **The bug is not the ladder. The bug is the price drift, and the registry closed the wrong
question about it.** The "no change needed" note was written on 21 Oct, when the drift had not yet
been detected. The drift was found on 08 Nov by `storefront-parity` — three of the flagship SKUs
charge more than the registry owns (R450 vs R325, R1,450 vs R1,250, R12,500 vs R8,400) — and the
registry's closure note was never reopened to meet it.

**Owner decision needed, and it is a money decision, not a documentation one:**
- **Fix the store** to the owned prices (R325 / R1,250 / R8,400) → the ladder becomes a real 4%
  discount and the customer gets the advertised price. **This is what the registry says is correct.**
- **Or bless the drift** (R450 / R1,450 / R12,500) → then the 7kg case is a **23.2% per-kg
  premium**, the "beats seven 1kg bags" claim on the page is false, and the 7kg case should be
  **repriced down to about R10,150** to match the seven-bag comparison.

Right now we are shipping the **worst of both**: the store charges the drifted premium, while the
registry claims the case beats seven bags and that no change is needed.

**Blocked on:** `SHOPIFY_ADMIN_TOKEN` with `write_products` (the token in `.env` answers 401).
`ops/fix-shopify-price-drift.py` computes the exact edits and is dry-run by default.

**Not yet owned by a checker.** `storefront-parity` compares *live prices against owned prices* — it
does not evaluate *per-kg ladder logic*, so a case that is internally inconsistent with its own
single-unit price would pass it. **This is the gap.**

## 3. SA biltong: our per-kg band, and where the ceiling is

Context for the price-ladder decision above — where R1,200–1,450/kg sits against the market.

- **Woolworths** — R157.99 per 150g chilli-beef biltong snapstick = **R1,053/kg** (Newsday, 15 Jul 2026).
- **SA retail dry beef biltong** — from **R235/kg**; South Africa's meat CPI **+12.6%** y/y to Dec 2026
  (growthmarketreports, 28 Oct 2026).
- **SA beef input** — **US$6.66/kg (R129)** as of Nov 2026, **+15.8% y/y**; 18,887 t exported
  (selinawamucii).
- **Conversion reality** — ~3kg fresh meat yields 1kg biltong, so at R120/kg raw and no spice or
  labour, the floor is around **R360/kg** (Reddit r/south_africa practitioner thread — treat as
  indicative, not a citation).
- **Game biltong** — **R200/kg and up** (Vans, Cape Town, Nov 2026).

**Read:** our owned ladder (**R1,200–1,250/kg**, 7kg case R8,400) sits **above** Woolworths'
R1,053/kg benchmark and **~4×** the R235/kg SA retail floor, in a market where input costs are up
~16% y/y. That is a defensible premium for a Wagyu-graded, CITES-permit-backed product — **and it
only holds if the product we sell is the product we describe.** At the drifted live prices
(R1,450–1,786/kg) we are asking for a larger premium with the same evidence, and the 7kg case asks
the customer for **23% more per kg** than the single bags they can buy instead. Nobody needs a
machine to see that; it needs a checker, which is tonight's build.
